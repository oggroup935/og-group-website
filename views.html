Straight talk about the number

A cash offer is lower than a listing. Here's the trade.

Anyone who tells you a cash offer matches what a fixed-up house sells for on the open market is not being straight with you. It doesn't, and it isn't supposed to.

What you're trading price for is everything else: no repairs coming out of your pocket, no commission, no closing costs, no months of taxes and insurance while it sits, no buyer's financing falling apart two weeks before closing, and a date you choose. For some people that trade is clearly worth it. For others it isn't — and if we think you'd do better listing it, we'll say so.

When we send your offer, the comparable sales and the repair estimate come with it. You'll see the reasoning, not just the number.

The process

From first call to closing day.

No part of this is a mystery. Here is every step, what we need from you at each one, and roughly how long it takes. You can stop at any point, for any reason.

1

You reach out

The form, a phone call, or a text — whatever's easiest. We want the address, a rough sense of the condition, and how quickly you'd like to be done. Nothing you say here commits you to anything.

2 minutes · nothing binding
2

We look up the house

Before we ever call you back, we pull what similar houses nearby actually sold for in recent months, check the county records, taxes and lot, and get a first read on the work it likely needs. We'd rather come to the call prepared than ask you twenty questions.

Same day, usually
3

One conversation

A short call so we understand things a database can't: what's been fixed, what hasn't, who's living there, what matters to you about the timing. If it's easier, send photos or a quick video walk-through instead — many people do.

15–20 minutes

A walk-through, if it helps

For most houses we'll want to see it once, in person, so the offer is solid rather than hedged. It takes half an hour, you don't need to clean or prepare anything, and there's no one to impress. If you'd rather not, we can work from photos with a wider margin.

30 minutes · optional
5

Your written offer

A specific number, in writing, with the comparable sales we used and the repair estimate we built. Read it, sleep on it, run it by your family or your attorney. Ask us why any line is what it is — we'll answer.

Typically 24–48 hours after the visit
6

You decide

Yes, no, or "not yet." If it's no, that's the end of it — we won't hound you. If it's yes, we sign a simple purchase agreement and hand it to a local title company.

Why we do it this way

This corner of the business earned its bad reputation.

Homeowners get postcards from strangers, calls from out-of-state numbers, and offers that quietly drop by twenty thousand dollars a week before closing. Nobody explains where the number came from, because explaining it would make it harder to defend.

We decided to run the opposite way: do the homework properly, put the reasoning in the seller's hands along with the price, and never move the number after we've agreed on it. It's slower. It also means the people we buy from tend to send their neighbors to us.

01

Truth, even when it costs us

If listing the house would net you more and you can wait, we'll tell you — and we've told people exactly that. An offer we can't explain isn't one we'll make.

02

The price we say is the price you get

No renegotiating on the courthouse steps, no surprise deductions, no fees appearing at closing. If something genuinely unexpected turns up, we tell you the moment we find it.

03

Respect for the situation

Behind most of these houses is an inheritance, an illness, a divorce, or a plan that didn't work out. That deserves patience and privacy, not a sales script.

Questions

Answered straight.

Including the uncomfortable ones. If something you want to know isn't here, call and ask — we'd rather answer it now than have you wondering.

How much is this going to cost me?+
Nothing. No fees, no commissions, no closing costs on your side, and no charge if you decide not to sell. The only money that moves is money moving to you.
How do you come up with the number?+
Two things: what comparable houses near you actually sold for recently, and what it would cost to bring yours up to that condition. We work from the first, subtract the second and the cost of carrying and reselling the house, and that's the offer. When we send it, the comparable sales and the repair estimate come with it so you can check our work.
Will you buy it in the condition it's in?+
Yes — that's the entire point. Roof, furnace, mold, fire damage, a full basement of belongings, a hoarding situation, code violations. Don't repair anything, don't clean anything out. Take what you want and leave the rest.
Is your offer as much as I'd get listing with an agent?+
No, and anyone claiming otherwise isn't being straight with you. A listed house that's been repaired and staged sells for more. What you trade that difference for is no repairs, no commission, no closing costs, no months of taxes and utilities while it sits, no showings, and no risk of a buyer's loan falling through. If your house is in good shape and you have time to wait, listing it will probably net you more — and we'll tell you that.
Do you actually buy it, or do you sell my contract to someone else?+
Both happen. Some houses we buy and renovate ourselves; for others we bring in a vetted buying partner who takes it on. Either way, you sign one agreement, at one price, and that price does not change — and we tell you which it is rather than hiding it. Your closing is handled by a neutral local title company regardless.
What if I owe back taxes, or there's a lien?+
Very common, and usually not a dealbreaker. Liens, back taxes and unpaid utilities are typically cleared out of the sale proceeds at closing by the title company. Just tell us what you know about — surprises are the only real problem.
The house is in probate / I inherited it with my siblings.+
Also common. We can usually work alongside an ongoing probate, and we're used to deals where several family members have to agree. We'll talk to your attorney directly if that makes it easier for everyone.
There are tenants living there.+
Fine. You don't have to evict anyone or wait for a lease to run out — we buy occupied properties and take on the tenancy as it stands. Please just tell us the real situation, including if they've stopped paying.
How fast can this be done?+
Typically two to four weeks from a signed agreement, most of which is the title company doing its work. If you need longer — you're waiting on a new place, a school year, or probate — we'll set the closing whenever suits you.
Am I obligated once I get an offer?+
No. An offer is a proposal, not a contract. Nothing is binding until both sides sign a purchase agreement, and you're free to say no at any point before that without a reason.
Will my house be listed publicly or advertised?+
No. It never goes on the MLS, and we don't post your address, photos or situation anywhere public. Neighbors, tenants and relatives find out from you, not from a sign in the yard.
What happens to my phone number?+
It's used to reach you about your property and nothing else. We don't sell it, trade it, or hand it to a list of other buyers. Say no and we stop contacting you — that's a rule, not a courtesy.
Do I have to be there for the closing?+
Usually not. Out-of-state sellers close remotely all the time — the title company can arrange a mobile notary or a remote signing and wire the funds to your account.
Who am I actually signing with — a company, or a person?+
A company. OG GROUP is a registered limited liability company (LLC) in the State of Ohio, with a registered agent and a public filing you can look up yourself on the Ohio Secretary of State's business search. The purchase agreement is between you and that company — not a name written on a postcard — and the money moves through a neutral, licensed Ohio title company that holds the file and disburses the funds at closing. If you want our registered entity name and filing number before you sign anything, or before you even tell us your address, ask and we'll send it.
Are you a licensed real-estate agent?+
No, and we're not acting as one. We're a registered Ohio LLC buying property for our own account — a buyer, not a broker. We're not representing you, listing your property, or charging you a commission. We can't give you legal or tax advice either — for those, please talk to your own attorney or accountant, and we're happy to work with them.
